Portobello

Edinburgh's beach & promenade, right outside the gate: two miles of sand and the Victorian prom, with water sports, saunas, cafés, independent shops and a long history as the city's playground.

It's one of Scotland's best-known sea-swimming beaches, on the Firth of Forth, where the river widens into the North Sea. Whatever the weather, swimmers are in at dawn, at lunch and at sunset, and The Annex is right in the middle of it.

Edinburgh city centre is 3 miles away, 7 minutes by train. Spend the day in the city, then come home to the sea. Explore Edinburgh

Eat, drink and play

Local food shops

St Andrews, a seaside chippy for around a century, voted Edinburgh's best by Evening News readers. Findlays, the butcher famous for its award-winning haggis. Root Down, an organic greengrocer on the High Street.

On the prom

Cafés with sea views, including The Beach House and Miro's. Down by Figgate Lane there's street food from ShrimpWreck and Civerinos, and Crumbs for a breakfast roll with Findlays haggis.

Tapas and gelato

Malvarosa on the High Street serves proper Spanish tapas and paella. For afters, award-winning Oscar's Gelato, crowned UK national champion, has a shop on the prom and a gelateria on Brighton Place.

Bars

The Espy right on the prom, and the Portobello Tap (the Porty Tap to locals) on the High Street, with craft beer and a beer garden.

Fine dining in Leith

About three miles along the coast, Leith has one of Scotland's best restaurant scenes, including Tom Kitchin's Michelin-starred The Kitchin.

The Beach

Two miles of sand, used for everything from beach volleyball by the Swim Centre to sailing, rowing, paddleboarding and kayaking off the shore. It's an officially recognised bathing beach, and you'll find swimmers in the sea all year round.

Soul Water Sauna

Edinburgh's first wood-fired sauna sits on the promenade, about 100 metres from the door. Book a slot, dip in the sea, warm up, go again.

Victorian Turkish baths

Portobello Swim Centre, built in 1898, still has its original Turkish baths and steam room, along with an indoor pool for days the sea is too wild.

Portobello's seaside past

The pier, 1871 to 1917

A 1,250 ft pleasure pier ran out into the Forth from the end of Bath Street, with a pavilion, restaurant and concert hall at its head. It was designed by Sir Thomas Bouch, engineer of the ill-fated Tay Bridge, and was demolished in 1917, its iron supports too rusted to be worth repairing.

Old photos and postcards of the pier

The open-air pool, 1936 to 1979

Portobello's Art Deco bathing pool had Scotland's first wave machine, diving boards and grandstands. Its seawater was heated by the cooling water from Portobello Power Station next door. Queues stretched along Westbank Street on summer days, and a young Sean Connery worked there as a lifeguard. It closed in 1979.

Archive photos of the pool

The swimmers today

The pier and pool are gone, but the swimming never stopped. Year-round sea swimmers carry on the tradition from the same beach, a few steps from The Annex's gate.
