A house with a story

Pohutukawa House was built in 1869, designed by James Campbell Walker, a leading Edinburgh architect better known for schools and civic buildings. It was one of three villas he built side by side on the Portobello waterfront, each in an eclectic Baronial style and each a different design.

Today it's Category B listed and sits within the Portobello Conservation Area. Its listing calls it Rogue Baronial, a fair description of all that turret, carving and invention.

For a century it was Beachborough House, until it became the Temple Hall Hotel in the 1970s. Locals may remember it as the hotel, or later as the Dalriada bar. Now it's a home again, and The Annex is its newest chapter.

The house appears on the map

The same stretch of Joppa shore on Ordnance Survey maps, from open fields before the villa was built to the streets of the 1930s. Hamilton Street, beside the house on the older maps, is today's Brunstane Road North, renamed around the middle of the 20th century.

The original layout

This large-scale Ordnance Survey town plan shows the house as first built, between the promenade and Bedford Place. The street was renamed Bedford Terrace some time between 1905 and 1913, as the maps below show. The cross-hatched shapes are glasshouses: a greenhouse in the garden and a conservatory against the house.

The gardens, boundary walls and gate to the promenade are all drawn in, along with the lamp posts on the prom.
